# Interface directions

Three full-window mocks of the same beat. The desktop app is built from **A. Studio desk**, because that layout keeps all eight tracks visible while a pattern is programmed. B and C stay here as the other choices.

## A. Studio desk

`a-studio-desk.jpg`

Dark transport, a named track column with mute and solo, a 16-step grid with a playhead on the steps, and an inspector for note, gate, and velocity.

On a phone the track column, the grid, and the inspector sit in three columns that do not fit. A narrow layout would stack the inspector under the grid and shorten the track names.

## B. Machine

`b-machine.jpg`

One voice at a time and sixteen large pads. This is the fastest way to program a single drum and the layout that can shrink: the pads wrap to two rows of eight.

You cannot see the kick and the hat together. Comparing voices means tapping between them.

## C. Daylight Hits

`c-daylight.jpg`

The original light 8 by 16 grid, with names, beat groups, and the playhead drawn on the steps.

On a phone it has the same width problem as A, with mute and solo text added. It would need horizontal scrolling or a one-voice mode.
